Critter Crunch

Capybara’s puzzle game lets Biggs turn a canopy full of critters into one long food chain.[1][2][3]

In this entry

The gameDevelopmentPeople & studiosRelease & receptionFiles & versionsSources

The game

Biggs grabs smaller creatures with his tongue and shoots them into the mouths of bigger ones. When a critter gulps down its fill and bursts, matching neighbours can go with it. The game grew from an original mobile project at Toronto’s Capybara Games.[1][2][3]

Development

Lead programmer Radek Koncewicz built the prototype to try out rules and test the limits of the phones. At first, two neighbouring critters would only chain together if they had eaten the same amount of food. Playtesters had trouble keeping track. Koncewicz proposed chaining by colour alone, which made the board easier to read and sped up play.[3]

Koncewicz built two level editors and a Photoshop export script and wrote a guide for outside teams porting the game. Vic Nguyen drew and animated the characters and David Hoang worked on the backgrounds. Kenneth Yeung wrote procedural effects. The studio also built its own tool to automate part of the move from Java to BREW.[3][2][4]

Release & reception

The mobile game won Best Game and Audio Achievement at the first IGF Mobile awards in February 2008. Capy co-founder Nathan Vella said carriers had initially passed it over. Verizon picked it up and featured it after the awards.[5][6]

Edition & research notes

MobyGames dates BREW to 2007. Vella places Verizon’s pickup after the February 2008 awards. The first day of BREW availability and the team behind this specific build remain unconfirmed. The surviving named credits cover the original mobile production and Java edition.[4][7][6]
