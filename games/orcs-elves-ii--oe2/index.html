Orcs & Elves II

A thief steals a talking wand to save his village in Fountainhead’s second first-person RPG.[1][2][3]

In this entry

The game

Valin needs the wand Ellon to save his village, and Ellon would rather be back with his previous owner. In the first-person, turn-based sequel, you take Valin through dwarven towns, caverns and haunted forests. You can also take control of his pet mouse, Bob, and send him into spaces a thief won’t fit.[1][2][3]

Development

The setting grew out of tabletop adventures John Carmack made for Katherine Anna Kang. By the time of the sequel, the pair had already worked with Fountainhead on DOOM RPG and the first Orcs & Elves. Kang described their working relationship in an October 2007 interview. Carmack would write the engine and spell out what the hardware could handle with the programmers, then Kang developed the design, story and characters within those limits.[4][5]

Kang was running id Mobile by November 2007. The Java sequel credits name her and Carmack for the original story. They also credit artists Matthew C. Ross and Sarah Sage, with Ross and Rich Douglas sharing music and sound.[6][7]

Release & reception

EA announced Orcs & Elves II for phones in July 2007 alongside the Nintendo DS edition of the first game. On December 3 it reported that the sequel was available on most North American carriers, with Europe due in early 2008. The preserved build identifies itself internally as orcsandelves2.[5][1][8]

Patrick Gann finished the game in about six hours for RPGFan. His low-end handset struggled with the graphics, but he singled out the additions to the first-person dungeon crawling, including picking locks, breaking out of prison and controlling Bob.[3]

Pocket Gamer’s Java review found that simple shortcuts left room for a full inventory and side quests. The 0 key opened the map, inventory and quest log, and two buttons cycled through weapons.[9]
