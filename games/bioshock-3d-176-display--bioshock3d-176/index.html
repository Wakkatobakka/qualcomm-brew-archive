BioShock 3D (176 display)

Studio Tridev brought Rapture to BREW using its ICE 3D engine.[1][2]

The game

The phone version of BioShock put a first-person Rapture on BREW. IG Fun announced the rights in February 2008, and by that May CEO Sean Malatesta had brought a demonstration to the BREW conference. It ran without player input. Malatesta said the phone was drawing the scene live. A separate 2D edition was in the works for phones that could not run it.[1][2]

Development

The 3D credits identify Studio Tridev as developer and Indiagames as distributor. Arjun Nair produced the project, Chaithanya K Singamala led programming and Poornima Seetharaman led game design. At GDC in March 2009, Malatesta described working with phones that could handle just one audio channel and had different graphics capabilities. The team managed to reproduce dynamic lighting in some parts of the 3D build.[3][4]

Lead designer Poornima Seetharaman recalled that 2K shared the original design document and some art while Studio Tridev rebuilt the game in C/C++. The team switched from Mascot to its own ICE 3D engine to get better performance. She used doors and the direction the player was facing to control when enemies loaded. She also pitched cutting Arcadia to 2K, which signed off on the change.[5]

Engine developer Pradip Bhattacharya recalled that the LG Voyager could use hardware acceleration. For phones without a dedicated graphics processor, the team supplied a software renderer using the phone’s CPU. On the creative side, Nitin Dhamse’s later portfolio and reel show models and animations from the project, including the Breadwinner Splicer and Little Sisters.[5][6][7]

Hidden Palace catalogs a level editor and map files from both parts of the project. A May 2009 build documents development of the first part. The separate August 2009 prototype preserves work on Part 2, which the preservation record lists as unreleased.[8][9][10]

Release & reception

The Verizon BREW game arrived in February 2010. MobyGames and Hidden Palace give February 8, while the BioShock Wiki gives February 9. The released adaptation ends at Smuggler’s Hideout. The second part continued the story in levels such as Fort Frolic, which are documented from an unreleased prototype.[11][8][5][10]

Edition & research notes

The 176 label distinguishes this RAR from the companion ZIP. It has the same seven numbered bio data files and also lists savedata.dat. The label and saved data alone do not provide a handset or a build number.[12][13]

  • Neither package has yet been matched byte-for-byte to a named Verizon device build.
  • No launch-period hands-on for the released BREW build was located. Show-floor footage, Java reviews and later prototype captures describe different evidence.
  • Level maps and the editor are cataloged at Hidden Palace. Its download link was unavailable in this check.
