Bowling 3D

JAMDAT added camera angles and a disco alley to its phone bowling game.[1]

In this entry

The gameDevelopmentPeople & studiosRelease & receptionFiles & versionsSources

The game

A shot starts with four decisions made on timing meters: where to stand, where to aim, how much power to use and how much spin to put on the ball. The 3D edition lets players replay a shot from different angles using the number keys. One of its three alleys has disco lights.[1]

Development

JAMDAT described Bowling 3D in its annual report as a new edition for high-end phones. Its earlier Bowling 2 already supported playing over the network. GameSpot found that the new game discarded network matches and kept local pass-and-play, despite adding replay and a better sense of being in an actual bowling alley.[2][1]

Release & reception

GameSpot reviewed Bowling 3D on the LG VX8000 in February 2005, calling it one of the handset’s launch games. Avery Score liked the replays and the distant sound of other lanes, but thought the omission of online play made the older Bowling 2 a better choice for returning players.[1]

Edition & research notes

The executable and four bowling-resource filenames match the 176-labelled package, which explicitly carries “jamdatbowling3d” in its save filename. JBowling3D preserves app.bar where the 176-labelled copy lists bowling3d.rb. The difference establishes separate file inventories, with exact handset assignments still open.[3][4]
