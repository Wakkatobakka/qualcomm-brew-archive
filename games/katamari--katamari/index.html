Rolling with Katamari

The Prince starts inside a phone, rolling up microchips and transistors before taking his katamari outside.[1][2]

The game

The Prince rolls a ball that picks up anything small enough to stick. Grow it past the size of a dog or a television and those become fair game, too. Rolling with Katamari brings that loop to isometric rooms and neighbourhoods. Its tutorial takes place inside the phone, where the first things to collect are microchips and transistors.[1][2]

Development

Keita Takahashi traced the original PlayStation 2 game to a prototype collaboration between Namco and the Digital Hollywood school. Namco’s team handled design, code and art direction. The students made the objects players could roll up. For the Western phone game, Namco Networks advertised 30 levels, including ones made specifically for phones.[3][4]

Release & reception

Namco Networks announced Rolling with Katamari for selected North American carriers in November 2008 and planned seasonal downloads on supporting phones. Reviewing the Java edition, Jon Mundy enjoyed the little environments and the King’s dialogue, though he found the steering became touchy as the ball grew.[4][1]

Japan had more than one keypad version. The 2D Katamari-kun came to Namco EZ Games in November 2007. At Tokyo Game Show in October 2008, KDDI separately showed the 3D Katamari Mobile for au. Directional keys steered and repeated presses of the centre button triggered the Prince Dash. Bandai Namco announced its release for April 30, 2009, through the ¥315-a-month Namco EZ Games service.[5][6][7][8]
