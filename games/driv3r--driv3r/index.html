DRIV3R

Sorrent gave Tanner his own top-down city chases for mobile phones.[1][2]

In this entry

The gameDevelopmentPeople & studiosRelease & receptionFiles & versionsSources

The game

An undercover cop with a getaway driver’s cover story, Tanner races the clock, rams rivals and can trade a wrecked car for whatever’s passing. The phone adaptation puts its take on the console game’s Miami, Nice and Istanbul under an overhead camera.[1][2]

Development

Sorrent’s Jill Braff said the studio would make the phone game in-house and market it alongside Atari’s console release. A June 2004 preview found that a tap nudged the car into the next lane, while holding the same button turned the car according to its speed. That gave a single direction key two jobs.[3][1]

Release & reception

Sorrent’s June 2004 Java release preceded its merger and rebrand with Macrospace. Pocket Gamer reviewed the Java game under the Glu Mobile name a year later. Mike Abolins liked being able to steal anything from a motorbike to a police cruiser and found the missions repetitive.[4][5][2]
