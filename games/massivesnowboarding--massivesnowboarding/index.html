Massive Snowboarding

Gameloft’s downhill game used the camera to turn tricks into a separate challenge.[1]

In this entry

The gameDevelopmentPeople & studiosRelease & receptionGalleryFiles & versionsSources

The game

Eight courses give each boarder three jobs: complete a trick checklist, beat a rival downhill, and perform for the press. In the filming challenges, the view switches to the camera crews as you jump. Some jumps cut between several cameras before you land.[1]

Development

Avery Score’s September 2005 review describes software-rendered graphics on the LG VX8000. He singled out the wipeouts, where animated tumbles and trails left in the snow gave crashes a convincing sense of weight. Jumping and grabs shared the 5 key, with the directional pad used for steering and tricks.[1]

Release & reception

Verizon’s V Cast edition was reviewed in September 2005. In December 2006, Telecom Italia announced a native BREW release in its first lineup of premium 3D games. TIM offered demos with the Samsung Z630. Qualcomm’s launch announcement leaves the regional origin of this archive package open.[1][2][3][4]
