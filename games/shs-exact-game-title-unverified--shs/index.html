Surviving High School

Centerscore’s school drama became an episodic game when its main story exceeded Verizon’s download limit.[1][2]

In this entry

The gameDevelopmentPeople & studiosRelease & receptionFiles & versionsSources

The game

A transfer student talks, dates and plays football while trying to fit in at a new school. Centerscore used dialogue choices to steer the story to different endings. The preserved phone descriptor names Surviving High School, Centerscore and Vivendi Games Mobile.[1][2]

Development

Design director Leighton Kan’s first version, High School Kings, had players level up by fighting bullies and roam the building for side quests. Winston She proposed turning it into a story with choices at particular points. Football took over for combat and dating for collecting items. The working title became High School Chronicles.[1]

With the Verizon launch approaching, the studio learned its complete story was too large for a single download. The team already had a system for delivering small extra episodes. They split up the main story into pieces that could use that system and put it on sale as Surviving High School.[1]

Release & reception

The Game Developer retrospective puts the Verizon release in early 2006. Centerscore moved from episodes every three weeks to every week as subscriptions grew, then used a new scripting language to cut the sequel’s main pack down to size.[1]
